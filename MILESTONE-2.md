# Milestone 2: two explorers, a bond and a challenge

Launch Claim Checker was accepted as a Project in its first version: a contract that says what two pages show about a launch claim. The first milestone added claim bounties (a second contract, [`MILESTONE.md`](MILESTONE.md)). This milestone is version 2 of the checker itself: **a launch claim is read on two block explorers on different domains, a verdict stands only when both show the same thing, and money can be put behind a verdict and challenged.**

## What the contract does

1. **Check.** `check_claim(explorer_a_url, explorer_b_url, coin_name, claim_type, claim_text)`. Every validator renders both pages itself, in two separate model calls. On each page code first checks that the coin's name stands near the top; the model fills a fixed form; plain Python checks every value against that validator's own copy (block number printed, amount exact on the copied line, catch-all miner rows refused). Then the two pages are combined: both contradict by the same rule (same block, same amount) → `CONTRADICTED`; both support by the same rule → `SUPPORTED`; one each way → `UNDETERMINED`, `D1_EXPLORERS_DISAGREE`.
2. **Bond.** Within the bond window the requester can bond a verdict about a coin's earliest blocks that both explorers agree on (`BOTH:P1` or `BOTH:P2`). Mining verdicts change from block to block and cannot be bonded.
3. **Challenge.** Until the challenge window closes, anyone except the requester can deposit exactly the bond, once per address. No cap on the number of challengers.
4. **Re-check.** In a fixed window after a grace period, anyone can have the validators read both pages again under the same rules. Same verdict: **upheld**, the requester collects the bond and every deposit. The opposite verdict: **overturned**, each challenger collects its deposit and an equal share of the bond. Anything else, or no re-check in time: **not confirmed**, everyone collects their own money. A bond nobody challenged is **released**.
5. **Collect and withdraw.** Pull payments only. `bond` and `challenge` run no model and never raise: a refused payment is credited straight back.

Windows are constructor settings with a floor of 10 minutes each, no owner and no setter: by default 60 minutes to bond, 24 hours to challenge, 60 minutes of grace, 60 minutes to re-check. Every deployment named here uses the 10-minute floor, so a whole case runs in about 40 minutes. The full rules, the interface and the limits are in the [README](README.md#version-2-two-explorers-a-bond-and-a-challenge).

## How it was checked

**Tests.** `tests/direct/test_checker_v2.py`: 70 direct-mode tests, passing on GenVM v0.2 (Studio) and on v0.6 against the packed Bradbury build (`tools/test_v6_v2.sh`). `tools/mutate_v2.py` breaks the contract in 93 ways, one at a time, and the tests catch all 93 (four further changes are listed in the script as equivalent, with the reason). Covered: every rule with its receipts; the explorers disagreeing; wrong pages; a planted instruction; fabricated lines and amounts; leaders that lie (another page's line, another amount, another miner, a verdict that does not follow from its pages); validators whose copy is one block later; every refused input; the domain rule; every time boundary to the second; the bond share and its remainder; a seeded random walk that keeps every wei accounted for. The accepted checker's 62 tests and the bounties' 419 still pass.

**Review.** The first design went to a separate AI reviewer session (Claude) that had not seen it being written. Verdict: not ready, six blockers; each is fixed in what was built. [`reviews/v2/README.md`](reviews/v2/README.md) records them. The contract was built by an AI session as well, working for Brilliant Nodes. No person outside Brilliant Nodes and no other model has reviewed it.

**Two explorers, two domains.** Explorer A, "Examplescan", is a set of test pages on checker.brilliantnodes.com. Explorer B, "Blockfield", is a second test explorer with a different layout, in its own repository and on another domain: [brilliantnodes.github.io/demo-explorer](https://brilliantnodes.github.io/demo-explorer/) ([source](https://github.com/brilliantnodes/demo-explorer)). Both are generated by [`tools/make_v2_pages.py`](tools/make_v2_pages.py). To show an overturn, both explorers "correct" a coin's block 1 after the bond; to show a not-confirmed outcome, only one does. Each correction is a commit in both repositories, recorded in the live-run files with the time it went live.

## Limits

Stated in full in the [README](README.md#limits-of-version-2). In short: a check trusts its two pages, and two domains run by the same party are not two witnesses (the contract cannot tell who runs a domain; the domain rule is a short table, not the public suffix list); the rules are a model's reading with code checks, not a proof; P rules do not see allocations made in a chain's genesis state or blocks the page does not show; a re-check reads the pages as they are then, so a page that changed or an explorer that is down decides the outcome; mining verdicts cannot be bonded. Both explorers in the live runs are Brilliant Nodes' own test pages; one check on two real explorers (Litecoin's block 1 on blockchair.com and live.blockcypher.com) could not be read by the validators, so nothing was recorded. No outside party has bonded or challenged a verdict yet.
