# Milestone 2: two explorers, a bond and a challenge

Launch Claim Checker was accepted as a Project in its first version: a contract that says what two pages show about a launch claim. The first milestone added claim bounties (a second contract, [`MILESTONE.md`](MILESTONE.md)). This milestone is version 2 of the checker itself: **a launch claim is read on two block explorers on different domains, a verdict stands only when both show the same thing, and money can be put behind a verdict and challenged.**

## What the contract does

1. **Check.** `check_claim(explorer_a_url, explorer_b_url, coin_name, claim_type, claim_text)`. Every validator renders both pages itself, in two separate model calls. On each page code first checks that the coin's name stands near the top; the model fills a fixed form, and an answer out of form makes the page unreadable; plain Python checks every value against that validator's own copy (the block number printed on a line that says "block" or "height", the amount exact on the copied line, catch-all miner rows refused). Then the two pages are combined: both contradict by the same rule (same block and amount, or the same miner) → `CONTRADICTED`; both support by the same rule → `SUPPORTED`; one each way → `UNDETERMINED`, `D1_EXPLORERS_DISAGREE`. An undetermined result is stored in short form (the reason and each page's class and rule), and every validator compares all of it.
2. **Bond.** Within the bond window the requester can bond a `NO_PREMINE` or `NO_FOUNDER_REWARD` verdict that both explorers agree on (`BOTH:P1` or `BOTH:P2`) **about block 0 or 1**. Only there can the opposite reading (P2: the block pays nothing but the ordinary reward) exist, so a verdict on a later block could never be overturned. Mining verdicts change from block to block, and a `FAIR_LAUNCH` verdict can only ever be contradicted, so neither can be bonded.
3. **Challenge.** Until the challenge window closes, anyone except the requester can deposit exactly the bond, once per address. No cap on the number of challengers.
4. **Re-check.** In a fixed window after a grace period, anyone can have the validators read both pages again under the same rules. A reading decides only when both explorers show a definite fact or opposite facts:
   - the same fact (same rule; on both explorers the same block and, for P1, the same amount): **upheld**, the requester collects the bond and every deposit;
   - the opposite rule about the same block (`BOTH:P1` ↔ `BOTH:P2`): **overturned**, half the bond (rounded down) is burned, and each challenger collects its deposit and an equal share of the other half;
   - opposite facts on the two explorers (`D1`), or a definite reading of another block or amount: **not confirmed**, everyone collects their own money.

   Any other reading decides nothing and the window stays open: a page that cannot be read, a page without the coin, or a page that shows nothing that decides, as an error page that keeps the explorer's header does. With no decided re-check in time, `settle` ends the bond not confirmed; a bond nobody challenged is **released**. The first decided re-check is final.
5. **Collect and withdraw.** Pull payments only. `bond` and `challenge` run no model and never raise: a refused payment is credited straight back. `totals()` gives locked, credited and burned; their sum equals the contract's balance once every withdrawal's transfer has been made (GEN sent with a call that takes none stays outside it).

Why half is burned: without it, a requester could bond a false verdict and challenge it from addresses of its own, and get most of the bond back when it is overturned. With half burned it loses at least half, however many addresses it uses.

Windows are constructor settings with a floor of 10 minutes each, no owner and no setter: by default 60 minutes to bond, 24 hours to challenge, 60 minutes of grace, 60 minutes to re-check. The Studio deployments named here use the 10-minute floor for all four, so a whole case runs in about 40 minutes; the Bradbury deployment uses 10 minutes for the first three and 20 minutes to re-check, because Bradbury transactions take longer and may need sending again. The full rules, the interface and the limits are in the [README](README.md#version-2-two-explorers-a-bond-and-a-challenge).

## How it was checked

**Tests.** `tests/direct/test_checker_v2.py`, `test_checker_v2_review.py` and `test_checker_v2_review3.py`: 151 direct-mode tests, passing on GenVM v0.2 (Studio) and on v0.6 against the packed Bradbury builds: v0.6.0-rc10 for the Phase 2 form and v0.6.0-rc17, the release Bradbury Phase 3 runs, for the Phase 3 form (`tools/test_v6_v2.sh`). `tools/mutate_v2.py` breaks the contract in 128 ways, one at a time, and the tests catch all 128 (the changes that cannot alter behaviour are listed in the script, with the reason). Covered: every rule with its receipts; the explorers disagreeing; wrong pages and error pages; a planted instruction; fabricated lines and amounts; leaders that lie (another page's line, another amount, another miner, a verdict that does not follow from its pages, an undetermined result with other page classes); validators whose copy is one block later; every refused input, including generic coin names and block-page field names; which verdicts can be bonded; what a re-check decides and what it leaves open; the domain rule; every time boundary to the second; the half burn and the share of the rest, with up to 50 challengers; and a seeded random walk that keeps a separate account of every address's credit, takes every outcome, and keeps every wei accounted for. The accepted checker's 62 tests and the bounties' 419 still pass.

**Reviews.** Five separate AI reviewer sessions (Claude) in three rounds, none of which had seen the work being written. Round 1 reviewed the design: not ready, six blockers, each fixed in what was built. Round 2 reviewed the first build in two parts side by side: the contract, money and tests (not ready, three blockers) and every claim in the write-up against the code, the records and the chain (ready with changes). Round 3 reviewed the second build the same way: the contract (not ready, one blocker: a P1 verdict on a block from 2 to 100 could be bonded but never overturned) and the package (ready with changes, eleven text corrections). Every blocker was fixed, and the live runs below were made again on the final build. [`reviews/v2/README.md`](reviews/v2/README.md) records each finding and what was done. The contract was built by an AI session as well, working for Brilliant Nodes. No person outside Brilliant Nodes and no other model has reviewed it.

**Two explorers, two domains.** Explorer A, "Examplescan", is a set of test pages on checker.brilliantnodes.com. Explorer B, "Blockfield", is a second test explorer with a different layout, in its own repository and on another domain: [brilliantnodes.github.io/demo-explorer](https://brilliantnodes.github.io/demo-explorer/) ([source](https://github.com/brilliantnodes/demo-explorer)). Both are generated by [`tools/make_v2_pages.py`](tools/make_v2_pages.py). To show an overturn, both explorers "correct" a coin's block 1 after the bond; to show a not-confirmed outcome, only explorer B does. Each correction is a commit in the repository of each explorer that changes, recorded in the live-run files with the time both public pages showed it.

## Limits

Stated in full in the [README](README.md#limits-of-version-2). In short: a check trusts its two pages, and two domains run by the same party are not two witnesses (the contract cannot tell who runs a domain; the domain rule is a short table, not the public suffix list); the rules are a model's reading with code checks, not a proof; P rules do not see allocations made in a chain's genesis state or blocks the page does not show; a re-check reads the pages as they are then and the first decided one is final, so a correction must be on both explorers when the window opens; whoever controls one of the two explorers can keep a re-check from deciding or force not confirmed (everyone's money comes back); only block-0-or-1 verdicts on `NO_PREMINE` and `NO_FOUNDER_REWARD` can be bonded; "upheld with n challengers" can be staged by a requester from its own addresses; the live deployments' windows are 10 or 20 minutes, for demonstration. Both explorers in the live runs are Brilliant Nodes' own test pages; two checks on real explorers could not be read by the validators. No outside party has bonded or challenged a verdict yet.
