# Milestone 2: two explorers, a bond and a challenge

Launch Claim Checker was accepted as a Project in its first version: a contract that says what two pages show about a launch claim. The first milestone added claim bounties (a second contract, [`MILESTONE.md`](MILESTONE.md)). This milestone is version 2 of the checker itself: **a launch claim is read on two block explorers on different domains, a verdict stands only when both show the same thing, and money can be put behind a verdict and challenged.**

## What the contract does

1. **Check.** `check_claim(explorer_a_url, explorer_b_url, coin_name, claim_type, claim_text)`. Every validator renders both pages itself, in two separate model calls. On each page code first checks that the coin's name stands near the top; the model fills a fixed form, and an answer out of form makes the page unreadable; plain Python checks every value against that validator's own copy (the block number printed on a line that says "block" or "height", the amount exact on the copied line, catch-all miner rows refused). Then the two pages are combined: both contradict by the same rule (same block and amount, or the same miner) → `CONTRADICTED`; both support by the same rule → `SUPPORTED`; one each way → `UNDETERMINED`, `D1_EXPLORERS_DISAGREE`. An undetermined result is stored in short form (the reason and each page's class and rule), and every validator compares all of it.
2. **Bond.** Within the bond window the requester can bond a `NO_PREMINE` or `NO_FOUNDER_REWARD` verdict about a coin's first blocks that both explorers agree on (`BOTH:P1` or `BOTH:P2`). Mining verdicts change from block to block, and a `FAIR_LAUNCH` verdict can only ever be contradicted, so neither can be bonded.
3. **Challenge.** Until the challenge window closes, anyone except the requester can deposit exactly the bond, once per address. No cap on the number of challengers.
4. **Re-check.** In a fixed window after a grace period, anyone can have the validators read both pages again under the same rules. The same fact (same rule; on both explorers the same block and, for P1, the same amount): **upheld**, the requester collects the bond and every deposit. The opposite rule (`BOTH:P1` ↔ `BOTH:P2`): **overturned**, half the bond (rounded down) is burned, and each challenger collects its deposit and an equal share of the other half. Anything else, or no re-check in time: **not confirmed**, everyone collects their own money. A page that cannot be read, or does not show the coin (an error page), decides nothing: the window stays open. A bond nobody challenged is **released**.
5. **Collect and withdraw.** Pull payments only. `bond` and `challenge` run no model and never raise: a refused payment is credited straight back. `totals()` gives locked, credited and burned; their sum is the contract's balance.

Why half is burned: without it, a requester could bond a false verdict and challenge it from addresses of its own, and get most of the bond back when it is overturned. With half burned it loses at least half, however many addresses it uses.

Windows are constructor settings with a floor of 10 minutes each, no owner and no setter: by default 60 minutes to bond, 24 hours to challenge, 60 minutes of grace, 60 minutes to re-check. The Studio deployments named here use the 10-minute floor for all four, so a whole case runs in about 40 minutes; the Bradbury deployment uses 10 minutes for the first three and 20 minutes to re-check (why: see the Bradbury run below). The full rules, the interface and the limits are in the [README](README.md#version-2-two-explorers-a-bond-and-a-challenge).

## How it was checked

**Tests.** `tests/direct/test_checker_v2.py` and `tests/direct/test_checker_v2_review.py`: 131 direct-mode tests, passing on GenVM v0.2 (Studio) and on v0.6 against the packed Bradbury build (`tools/test_v6_v2.sh`). `tools/mutate_v2.py` breaks the contract in 119 ways, one at a time, and the tests catch all 119 (the changes that cannot alter behaviour are listed in the script, with the reason). Covered: every rule with its receipts; the explorers disagreeing; wrong pages; a planted instruction; fabricated lines and amounts; leaders that lie (another page's line, another amount, another miner, a verdict that does not follow from its pages, an undetermined result with another page class); validators whose copy is one block later; every refused input, including generic coin names; the domain rule; every time boundary to the second; the half burn and the share of the rest, with up to 50 challengers; and a seeded random walk that keeps a separate account of every address's credit, takes every outcome, and keeps every wei accounted for. The accepted checker's 62 tests and the bounties' 419 still pass.

**Reviews.** Two separate AI reviewer sessions (Claude) that had not seen the work being written. The first reviewed the design: not ready, six blockers, each fixed in what was built. The second reviewed the first build, in two parts run side by side: the contract, money and tests (not ready, three blockers), and every claim in the write-up, article, video and page against the code, the records and the chain (ready with changes). Every blocker was fixed, and the live runs below were made again on the fixed build. [`reviews/v2/README.md`](reviews/v2/README.md) records each finding and what was done. The contract was built by an AI session as well, working for Brilliant Nodes. No person outside Brilliant Nodes and no other model has reviewed it.

**Two explorers, two domains.** Explorer A, "Examplescan", is a set of test pages on checker.brilliantnodes.com. Explorer B, "Blockfield", is a second test explorer with a different layout, in its own repository and on another domain: [brilliantnodes.github.io/demo-explorer](https://brilliantnodes.github.io/demo-explorer/) ([source](https://github.com/brilliantnodes/demo-explorer)). Both are generated by [`tools/make_v2_pages.py`](tools/make_v2_pages.py). To show an overturn, both explorers "correct" a coin's block 1 after the bond; to show a not-confirmed outcome, only explorer B does. Each correction is a commit in the repository of each explorer that changes, recorded in the live-run files with the time both public pages showed it.

## Limits

Stated in full in the [README](README.md#limits-of-version-2). In short: a check trusts its two pages, and two domains run by the same party are not two witnesses (the contract cannot tell who runs a domain; the domain rule is a short table, not the public suffix list); the rules are a model's reading with code checks, not a proof; P rules do not see allocations made in a chain's genesis state or blocks the page does not show; a re-check reads the pages as they are then, so a page that changed decides the outcome, and whoever controls one of the two explorers can keep a re-check from deciding (the bond then ends not confirmed); only first-block verdicts on `NO_PREMINE` and `NO_FOUNDER_REWARD` can be bonded; the live deployments' windows are 10 or 20 minutes, for demonstration. Both explorers in the live runs are Brilliant Nodes' own test pages; two checks on real explorers could not be read by the validators. No outside party has bonded or challenged a verdict yet.
