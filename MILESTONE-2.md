# Milestone 2: two explorers, a bond and a challenge

> **What this milestone upgrades.** Launch Claim Checker was accepted as a Project on 16 Nov; the claim-bounties milestone added a second contract. This milestone upgrades the checker contract itself to version 2:
> 1. **Two explorers instead of one.** Every validator reads the claim on two block explorers on different domains; a verdict stands only when both show the same fact by the same rule, and explorers that disagree are recorded as such.
> 2. **A bond behind a verdict.** The requester can back a verdict about a coin's block 0 or 1 with GEN.
> 3. **A challenge.** Anyone can challenge with the same amount.
> 4. **A re-check that moves the money.** After the challenge window the validators read both explorers again: the same fact upholds the bond, the opposite verdict about the same block overturns it (half burned, the challengers share the rest), and anything else returns everyone's money.
> 5. **A new Bradbury deployment**, on Testnet Bradbury Phase 3.
>
> The accepted checker and the bounties contract are unchanged, byte for byte.

## What the contract does

1. **Check.** `check_claim(explorer_a_url, explorer_b_url, coin_name, claim_type, claim_text)`. Every validator renders both pages itself, in two separate model calls. On each page code first checks that the coin's name stands near the top; the model fills a fixed form, and an answer out of form makes the page unreadable; plain Python checks every value against that validator's own copy (the block number printed on a line that says "block" or "height", the amount exact on the copied line, catch-all miner rows refused). Then the two pages are combined: both contradict by the same rule (same block and amount, or the same miner) → `CONTRADICTED`; both support by the same rule → `SUPPORTED`; one each way → `UNDETERMINED`, `D1_EXPLORERS_DISAGREE`. An undetermined result is stored in short form (the reason and each page's class and rule), and every validator compares all of it.
2. **Bond.** Within the bond window the requester can bond a `NO_PREMINE` or `NO_FOUNDER_REWARD` verdict that both explorers agree on (`BOTH:P1` or `BOTH:P2`) **about block 0 or 1**, and a `BOTH:P1` verdict **only when both pages also show the block's ordinary reward**, not zero. The opposite reading (P2: the block pays nothing but the ordinary reward, shown on the page) exists only there, so a P1 verdict on a later block, or on a block whose pages show no reward, could never be overturned. The model copies that reward on each P1 page; code keeps it only when the model's line, or else the page's first line that says "reward", stands on that validator's own copy with that value on it. Mining verdicts change from block to block, and a `FAIR_LAUNCH` verdict can only ever be contradicted, so neither can be bonded.
3. **Challenge.** Until the challenge window closes, anyone except the requester can deposit exactly the bond, once per address. No cap on the number of challengers.
4. **Re-check.** In a fixed window after a grace period, anyone can have the validators read both pages again under the same rules. A reading decides only when both explorers show a definite fact or opposite facts:
   - the same fact (same rule; on both explorers the same block and, for P1, the same amount): **upheld**, the requester collects the bond and every deposit;
   - the opposite rule about the same block (`BOTH:P1` ↔ `BOTH:P2`): **overturned**, half the bond (rounded down) is burned, and each challenger collects its deposit and an equal share of the other half;
   - opposite facts on the two explorers (`D1`), or a definite reading of another block or amount: **not confirmed**, everyone collects their own money.

   Any other reading decides nothing and the window stays open: a page that cannot be read, a page without the coin, or a page that shows nothing that decides, as an error page that keeps the explorer's header does. With no decided re-check in time, `settle` ends the bond not confirmed; a bond nobody challenged is **released**. The first decided re-check is final.
5. **Collect and withdraw.** Pull payments only. `bond` and `challenge` run no model and never raise: a refused payment is credited straight back. `totals()` gives locked, credited and burned; their sum equals the contract's balance once every withdrawal's transfer has been made (GEN sent with a call that takes none stays outside it).

Why half is burned: without it, a requester could bond a false verdict and challenge it from addresses of its own, and get most of the bond back when it is overturned. With half burned it loses at least half, however many addresses it uses.

Windows are constructor settings with a floor of 10 minutes each, no owner and no setter: by default 60 minutes to bond, 24 hours to challenge, 60 minutes of grace, 60 minutes to re-check. The Studio deployments named here use the 10-minute floor for all four, so a whole case runs in about 40 minutes; the Bradbury deployment uses 10 minutes for the first three and 20 minutes to re-check, because Bradbury transactions take longer and may need sending again. The full rules, the interface and the limits are in the [README](README.md#version-2-two-explorers-a-bond-and-a-challenge).

## How it was checked

**Tests.** `tests/direct/test_checker_v2.py`, `test_checker_v2_review.py`, `test_checker_v2_review3.py` and `test_checker_v2_review4.py`: 183 direct-mode tests, passing on GenVM v0.2 (Studio) and on v0.6 against the packed Bradbury builds: v0.6.0-rc10 for the Phase 2 form and v0.6.0-rc17, the release Bradbury Phase 3 runs, for the Phase 3 form (`tools/test_v6_v2.sh`). `tools/mutate_v2.py` breaks the contract in 146 ways, one at a time, and the tests catch all 146 (the changes that cannot alter behaviour are listed in the script, with the reason). Covered: every rule with its receipts; the explorers disagreeing; wrong pages and error pages; a planted instruction; fabricated lines and amounts; leaders that lie (another page's line, another amount, another miner, a verdict that does not follow from its pages, an undetermined result with other page classes); validators whose copy is one block later; every refused input, including generic coin names and block-page field names; which verdicts can be bonded, including a P1 verdict whose pages show no reward, a reward of 0 or a reward on one page only, and the reward as models copy it ("50" for "50.00000000", "50 EXM", a line not on the page); what a re-check decides and what it leaves open; the domain rule; every time boundary to the second; the half burn and the share of the rest, with up to 50 challengers; and a seeded random walk that keeps a separate account of every address's credit, takes every outcome, and keeps every wei accounted for. The accepted checker's 62 tests and the bounties' 419 still pass.

**Reviews.** Seven separate AI reviewer sessions (Claude) in four rounds, none of which had seen the work being written. Round 1 reviewed the design: not ready, six blockers, each fixed in what was built. Round 2 reviewed the first build in two parts side by side: the contract, money and tests (not ready, three blockers) and every claim in the write-up against the code, the records and the chain (ready with changes). Round 3 reviewed the second build the same way: the contract (not ready, one blocker: a P1 verdict on a block from 2 to 100 could be bonded but never overturned) and the package (ready with changes, eleven text corrections). Round 4 reviewed the third build, with its Bradbury Phase 3 port, the same way: the contract (ready with changes: a P1 verdict on a block whose pages show no ordinary reward could be bonded but never overturned, now refused) and the package (ready with changes, eight corrections). Every blocker and must-fix was dealt with, and the live runs below were made again on the final build. [`reviews/v2/README.md`](reviews/v2/README.md) records each finding and what was done. The contract was built by an AI session as well, working for Brilliant Nodes. No person outside Brilliant Nodes and no other model has reviewed it.

## Limits

Stated in full in the [README](README.md#limits-of-version-2). In short: a check trusts its two pages, and two domains run by the same party are not two witnesses (the contract cannot tell who runs a domain; the domain rule is a short table, not the public suffix list); the rules are a model's reading with code checks, not a proof; P rules do not see allocations made in a chain's genesis state or blocks the page does not show; a re-check reads the pages as they are then and the first decided one is final, so a correction must be on both explorers when the window opens; whoever controls one of the two explorers can keep a re-check from deciding or force not confirmed (everyone's money comes back); only block-0-or-1 verdicts on `NO_PREMINE` and `NO_FOUNDER_REWARD` can be bonded, P1 ones only when both pages show the block's ordinary reward; "upheld with n challengers" can be staged by a requester from its own addresses; the live deployments' windows are 10 or 20 minutes, for demonstration. Both explorers in the live runs are Brilliant Nodes' own test pages; two checks on real explorers could not be read by the validators. No outside party has bonded or challenged a verdict yet.
